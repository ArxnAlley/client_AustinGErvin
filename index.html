Questions people ask first.

These answers describe how these matters generally work in Ohio. They are general information, not legal advice, and they cannot account for the facts of any particular case.

Do I need a lawyer before my first court date?

You are not required to have one. Whether you want one is a different question. The decisions made early — what you say at a first appearance, what you waive, what you agree to — are usually the hardest ones to undo later.

If you are deciding, describing the situation on the phone costs nothing, and you will be told plainly whether it is the kind of matter that needs representation.

What happens at an arraignment in Ohio?

It is short, and it is not a trial. The court states the charge, advises you of your rights, and asks for a plea — ordinarily not guilty, guilty, or no contest. The judge then addresses bond and any conditions of release, and sets the next date.

Most of what matters at an arraignment is what it sets in motion afterward rather than what happens in the room.

Will I lose my driver's license after an OVI arrest?

Not automatically, and not necessarily for good — but an arrest can begin an administrative suspension that starts before any conviction and runs separately from whatever a court decides later.

That suspension can be challenged, and limited driving privileges are sometimes available. The period for raising it is short and begins running from the arrest, which is why this is a question to ask within days rather than weeks.

What type of protection order do I need in Ohio?

Ohio has more than one, and which one fits turns on two things: what the other person is to you, and whether a criminal case already exists.

An order arising out of a family or household relationship is handled differently from one between people with no such relationship, and an order issued inside a pending criminal case is different again. Bring the facts — who the person is to you, what happened, and whether police or a court are already involved — and the right petition follows from them.

What is the difference between a contested and an uncontested divorce?

A divorce is contested when the parties do not agree on something the court has to decide — property, debt, parenting time, support — and the court resolves what is left open. It is uncontested when the terms are already settled and the court is being asked to approve them.

Ohio also offers dissolution, which is begun only after a complete agreement is reached and is not a divorce action at all. Which route is open depends on how much is genuinely agreed.

How is child custody decided?

Ohio courts speak of allocating parental rights and responsibilities rather than of custody, and the standard is the best interest of the child.

The statute sets out the factors a court weighs — among them the child’s relationship with each parent, how settled the child is at home and in school, the health of everyone involved, and each parent’s willingness to support the child’s relationship with the other. There is no presumption in favor of a mother or of a father.

What should I bring to a first meeting?

Anything with a date on it. Charging papers, citations, court notices, anything you have been served with, and any letter from a court, a prosecutor, an insurer, or another attorney.

A written timeline helps as well — what happened and when, in order. If a hearing is already scheduled, that date is the single most useful thing you can bring.

When does an estate need to go through probate?

It is usually a question of how property is titled rather than of how large the estate is. Assets that pass on their own — a joint account with survivorship, a payable-on-death or transfer-on-death designation, insurance with a named beneficiary, property already held in a trust — ordinarily pass outside probate.

What is left in the decedent’s name alone, with no beneficiary and no survivor on the title, is generally what probate exists to move. Ohio also has simplified procedures for smaller estates.

Describe the matter in your own words. What happened, when, and a number you can be reached on is enough to start.
