#### Legal marketing and ethics implications

Both Ohio and Kentucky regulate attorney advertising. Ohio’s Rule 7.2 permits public dissemination of information such as name, address, website, phone number, and service categories, while Rule 7.1 and related ethics guidance prohibit false or misleading communications. Kentucky’s attorney advertising is governed by SCR 3.130-7.01 through 7.60, and Kentucky ethics guidance treats websites as advertising subject to false-or-misleading constraints. That means the site should avoid unverifiable superlatives, misleading comparisons, unsupported “best” claims, unjustified expectations, and any implication that submitting a form alone creates an attorney-client relationship. citeturn33search1turn33search4turn33search8turn32search2turn32search16turn32search10

A prudent intake disclaimer should make clear that contacting the firm does not itself create an attorney-client relationship and that users should avoid sending confidential details until engagement is confirmed. Competitor sites already use general legal-information and non-reliance disclaimers for this reason. citeturn16view3turn16view6turn32search16turn33search8
